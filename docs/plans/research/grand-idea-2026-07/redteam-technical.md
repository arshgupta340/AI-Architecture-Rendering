# Technical red team — whole pipeline

**Headline:** The full chain is a wow-demo, not a product: errors compound multiplicatively across four stochastic stages, and the one deterministic asset (the Rhino mesh) is the only thing worth building the whole DAG around — cut video and generative-splat-authoring to research-track, keep mesh-anchored hero + composited splat backdrop as the shippable core.

## Where errors compound (the multiplicative chain)

The grand vision is a 7-stage pipeline: **real mesh → AI entourage → engine render → diffusion hero → video fly-through → splat reconstruction → editable splat env → copilot**. The fatal structural fact is that **stages 2, 4, 5, 6 are each independently stochastic and lossy, and they are wired in series.** Acceptance probability multiplies. If entourage lands 80% of the time, hero 70%, a geometry-faithful fly-through 40% (verified: no hosted model preserves building geometry across a camera path), and a clean editable-splat bake 50%, the end-to-end first-pass yield is ~0.8·0.7·0.4·0.5 ≈ **11%**. Every stage inherits the drift of the one before it — the fly-through drifts *on top of* an already-drifting per-view hero, and the splat is then reconstructed *from* those inconsistent frames, baking the compounded error into "editable" 3D that is now wrong in ways no single stage's QA would catch.

Worse, the stages don't share a light-transport model. A generated 2D cutout person, a Rodin mesh bench, a TripoSplat tree, the FLUX hero, and the WebGPU real-time scene each carry an **independent lighting signature**. These don't average out — they stack, and the verified archviz literature is unanimous that sophisticated viewers register the mismatch pre-consciously. So the compounding is both *geometric* (drift) and *photometric* (inconsistent light), and they hit the same frame.

## Where non-destructiveness breaks — the "move the window 2 ft" test

This is the load-bearing stress test and it **cleanly partitions the pipeline**:

- **Mesh layer (survives):** move the window in Rhino, it propagates deterministically to depth/canny/ID buffers, HDRIs, camera, entourage placement. This is the one stage that is truly non-destructive. Everything defensible lives here.
- **Diffusion hero (breaks softly):** must re-roll that view. Masked `region_edit` keeps *elsewhere* byte-stable, so a local window move is survivable per-view — but multi-view/full-360 re-rolls drift (project's own known weak point).
- **Video fly-through (breaks hard):** re-render BOTH keyframes traditionally, then regenerate the interpolated clip — a *new* stochastic draw that may not match the previously approved take. Verified: Spline Dynamics calls this "a fundamental limitation" for multi-round client work. AI video is **structurally destructive under revision.**
- **Baked splat env (breaks fatally):** a splat baked from hero frames is a photo-proxy, not a model. Moving the window means the splat is now *wrong* and must be **fully re-baked** (~10–12 min + re-capture + re-train). There is no cheap edit — Marble's own "edit" (Pano Edit) is diffusion-regenerate-the-world. You cannot paint a splat like a mesh; editing/relighting/object-insertion in splats is all verified research-stage (3DSceneEditor, DeferredGS, LightHarmony3D — none production).

**Survival strategy:** the mesh must remain the single source of truth for geometry at all times. Splat and video are *downstream renders of the mesh*, never the editable substrate. The moment you invert that — bake the splat and then try to edit *it* — you've thrown away the only non-destructive layer you had. The user's stated pillar (c), "Gaussian splat of the whole environment **as editable 3D**," is the part that does not exist as shippable tech and should be reframed to "splat backdrop composited around the always-mesh building."

## Minimal DAG that still delivers the wow

Strip to the stages that are (a) deterministic or (b) single-stochastic-hop with a mesh anchor:

**mesh (Rhino) → engine buffers (depth/canny/ID) → FLUX+ControlNet hero (masked region_edit) → [optional] Spark-composited static splat backdrop.**

That's it. This is already ~90% built and verified. It delivers the actual wow — a geometry-locked photoreal still an architect can direct-manipulate — with **one** stochastic hop (the hero) that is masked and re-editable. Add a **still turntable** (6–12 discrete pre-rendered hero angles the user clicks between) instead of a continuous video: it captures 80% of the fly-through wow, is per-view maskable, and sidesteps the frame-1-vs-frame-240 consistency wall entirely.

**Cut or defer:** continuous video fly-through (research-track: GEN3C/DepthDirector self-host on Modal — verified GEN3C has open weights but *zero* inference providers, so it's a build-it-yourself project, not an API call), editable-splat authoring (research-track), and generative entourage-as-splat (bad fit — bakes lighting, verified). **Reorder:** copilot comes *last* and *read-only-first* — it's a UX layer over the typed scene graph, and its mutation reliability (verified Figma agent: 42% full-page success, silently edits un-requested things) makes it a time-sink under deadline until the mesh command bus exists.

## The 5 hardest technical problems, ranked

**1. Multi-view / full-360 hero consistency — SOLVABLE-WITH-EFFORT, but the moat is closing.** The project's reproject-from-3D is verified working for nearby angles and is the *rigorous* answer. But I verified the cheap answer is catching up: Nano Banana Pro reference-image conditioning ("one base render + material swatches, 2–8 refs") gives practitioners "material consistency perfect" across a *small set* of angles — no proprietary 3D reproject needed. It still drifts on window proportions and unseen back sides. So: reproject wins the hard cases (full-360, dimension-critical), the foundation model wins the easy cases for free, and it ships inside Veras. Push reproject only as far as full-360 turntable; don't over-invest against a monthly-improving base model.

**2. Geometry-true video fly-through — RESEARCH-GRADE.** Verified: every hosted model (Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, Luma Ray3.2) hallucinates/warps building geometry across camera motion. Ray3.2's "Modify Video" knobs *loosen* geometry for restyle — they are not a lock. The only real fix is depth/point-cloud-conditioned V2V (GEN3C pattern), which has open weights but no hosted API and ~520s/5s-clip self-host cost. This is the FLUX+ControlNet approach extended to video, but the training/serving lift is a multi-month research project. Do **not** put it on the MVP critical path.

**3. Editable / relightable Gaussian-splat environment — RESEARCH-GRADE (and partly obsoleted by incumbents).** Verified twice over: (a) editing/relighting/object-insertion in splats is all research-stage; (b) **Chaos already shipped splat relighting** — V-Ray 7 U3 (Apr 2026) and Vantage 3.3.0 (Jun 13 2026) both do GS relighting + clipping-mask trim. So the "frozen-lighting splat" gap the researchers flagged as whitespace is *already closing inside the incumbent* for the relight+clip primitive. Genuine full authoring (place/remove arbitrary assets) remains open but is the highest-execution-risk bet in the whole vision. Ship splats only as *static composited backdrop*, exactly as the existing Spark path already does.

**4. Dimensional / photometric honesty across composited stages — SOLVABLE-NOW, but as a hard product rule not a feature.** Verified: generative splat backdrops *hallucinate* the surrounding streetscape (setbacks, neighbor massing, street widths); 3DGS geometric error is ~8cm ±11cm vs LiDAR, and generated worlds are worse. Interestingly, newer 2026 ISPRS work finds 3DGS *better* than photogrammetry on thin members and glass for *visual completeness* — but still inferior on *metric accuracy*. For an architect presenting to a planning board, a wrong neighbor-building height is a liability. Fix is cheap and non-negotiable: hard-label all generated/backdrop context "illustrative, not survey," ground to Genie 3 Street View or Photorealistic 3D Tiles for real sites, keep all dimensioned geometry in the mesh.

**5. A global transactional command/undo bus — SOLVABLE-NOW, but it's unbuilt foundational work the copilot silently depends on.** Verified from project state: there is NO global undo bus — just per-feature localStorage stacks. The entire "every AI edit is a re-editable non-destructive layer / Ctrl+Z works" promise requires first building one transactional command bus that *every* existing direct-manipulation UI action also routes through (or agent-undo and manual-undo diverge). The copilot's "thin MCP bridge" framing hides this. Build the bus **before** the copilot, or the non-destructive core value is a claim without a mechanism.

## Bottom line for the DAG

The compounding argument and the revision test converge on the same answer: **the mesh is the product; everything generative is a downstream, re-derivable render of it.** The minimal wow-delivering DAG is mesh → buffers → masked hero → optional static splat backdrop + still turntable. Video and editable-splat-authoring are research-track garnish, not pillars — and two of the researchers' three "whitespace" moats (splat relighting, multi-view consistency) are already eroding under Chaos/Nano-Banana. The single most valuable unbuilt piece is not any AI stage — it's the **transactional command bus** that makes "non-destructive" true, because that is the one thing incumbents' model-native anchoring (D5/Veras editing the live CAD file) does structurally and this tool currently does not.
