# pipeline-economics — research + stress-test (2026-07-03)

**Headline:** Self-hosted FLUX hero renders on Modal cost ~$0.01-0.02 each (confirmed); video generation dominates marginal cost ($0.40-4.50/clip via API, ~$0.03-0.15/clip if self-hosted Wan2.2 on Modal); a $29-49/mo per-seat SaaS tier can sustain 70-85%+ gross margin at 50 renders + 10 clips + 2 splat bakes/user-month — price near Veras ($29-59/mo) and D5 Pro ($30-38/mo), undercutting Enscape/Lumion (~$48-125/mo).

## Key facts

- Modal per-second list prices (modal.com/pricing): A100 80GB $0.000694/s (~$2.50/hr), H100 $0.001097/s (~$3.95/hr), H200 $0.001261/s (~$4.54/hr), B200 $0.001736/s (~$6.25/hr) — confirmed by 3 independent 2026 trackers
- Veo 3 API: $0.40/s standard, $0.15/s Fast (ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing, Jul 2026) — an 8s clip costs $3.20 (Standard) or $1.20 (Fast)
- Kling 3.0/O3 API: $0.075-0.15/s depending on resolution/audio (kling.ai/dev/pricing) — 5s 720p no-audio clip ~$0.38
- Seedance 2.0 API: $0.074-0.303/s across providers depending on resolution/speed tier (fal.ai, evolink.ai, wavespeed, verified Apr 2026)
- Wan 2.2 (Apache 2.0, self-hostable): 5s 720p clip takes ~523s on A100 / ~284s on H100 per community benchmark (runaihome.com) -> roughly $0.31-0.36 in raw Modal GPU-seconds if self-hosted
- Gaussian splat (splatfacto, 30k iterations): 8-25 min depending on GPU (RTX4090 ~12min, A100 ~10min, H100 ~8min) -> ~$0.25-0.30/bake on Modal A100 raw compute, per multiple 2026 sources
- Image-to-3D asset APIs: Meshy/Tripo ~$0.10-0.30/model; Rodin (Hyper3D) ~$0.50-1.50/model but requires $120/mo minimum subscription (3daistudio.com, Mar 2026)
- Claude API (platform.claude.com, verified Jul 2026): Sonnet 4.6 $3/$15 per M in/out tokens, Opus 4.8 $5/$25, Haiku 4.5 $1/$5, Fable 5 $10/$50
- Veras (Chaos/EvolveLAB) — closest direct competitor to this product's vision — prices $29-59/mo for 500-2000 image/video credits, effective ~$0.06-0.10/render to end user (chaos.com/veras, evolvelab.io)
- Incumbent renderer annual pricing (2026): Enscape Solo $574.80/yr, Enscape Premium $634.80/yr (chaos.com); Lumion Pro $1,149/yr, Lumion Studio $1,499/yr (lumion.com); D5 Render Pro $360/yr or $38/mo (d5render.com)
- FLUX.1 Dev/Pro image API: ~$0.02-0.03/image (Dev) to $0.04-0.06/image (Pro) on Fal/Replicate; FLUX.1 Schnell is Apache 2.0, self-hostable at near-zero cost
- GPU host comparison for scale-to-zero serving (2026): Fal.ai A100 $0.99/hr & H100 $1.89/hr (cheapest, but per-output billing for packaged models); RunPod A100 $0.66-2.17/hr; Replicate A100/H100 ~$5.04/hr (priciest, best warm-cache latency); Modal sits mid-pack on raw rate but offers true custom-container per-second billing

## Recommendation

Keep Modal as the GPU backbone for the custom FLUX/ControlNet/splat pipeline (best fit for bespoke containers, true scale-to-zero, already proven at ~$0.01-0.02/render); do not migrate to Fal/Replicate/RunPod for that workload. For video, default the product to Kling O3 720p or Veo 3 Fast rather than Veo 3 Standard — this single choice swings total video COGS by 3-8x and is the dominant lever on gross margin. Price the SaaS at $39-49/mo per seat for a tier covering ~50 renders + 8-10 video clips + 1-2 splat bakes, anchored directly against Veras ($29-59/mo) and D5 Pro ($38/mo) rather than against Enscape/Lumion's much higher legacy-seat pricing — this yields an estimated 73-82% gross margin at the stated usage profile. Treat self-hosting Wan2.2 on Modal as a later-stage cost optimization once video volume justifies the engineering lift, not an MVP requirement. Verify the disputed "3x/1.25x Modal non-preemptible multiplier" claim directly against Modal's docs before it enters any pricing model, since it appears nowhere else and may be inaccurate.

## Full report

## GPU infra: Modal vs alternatives (verified mid-2026 rates)

Modal's published per-second list prices (modal.com/pricing, cross-confirmed by 3 independent trackers): A100 80GB $0.000694/s (~$2.50/hr), H100 $0.001097/s (~$3.95/hr), H200 $0.001261/s (~$4.54/hr), B200 $0.001736/s (~$6.25/hr). This matches the project's own measured ~$0.01-0.02/hero-render on A100.

**Flag, not fact:** one source (alatirok.com) claims a "3x non-preemptible + 1.25x US-region" production multiplier pushing effective H100 to ~$14.81/hr. This conflicts with Modal's plain pricing page and other trackers, who quote list price as effective price. Verify directly against modal.com/pricing/docs before budgeting — if real, it changes economics for any always-on (non-scale-to-zero) component, though it should not affect scale-to-zero hero-render/video/splat jobs, which are inherently bursty/preemptible-friendly.

**Competitor GPU hosts** (gputracker.dev, hostfleet.net Apr 2026; computeprices.com Jun 2026):
- **Fal.ai**: A100 80GB $0.99/hr, H100 $1.89/hr — cheapest of the majors on raw rate, but bills per-output/per-model for packaged pipelines (not GPU-second for custom code); ~100ms cold starts via "ParaAttention." Great fit for calling their curated FLUX/Kling/Veo endpoints; weaker fit for hosting the bespoke FLUX+ControlNet-Union pipeline this project already runs, unless ported to their custom-deployment tier.
- **RunPod**: A100 $0.66-2.17/hr (pod vs serverless), H100 $1.40-3.35/hr; per-second/per-ms serverless billing; container cold starts 20-60s (slower than Modal/Replicate) unless kept warm.
- **Replicate**: A100 80GB ~$5.04/hr, H100 ~$5.04/hr — most expensive of the four but fastest warm-cache re-hits (2-5s) and zero setup fee; per-prediction billing can obscure true GPU-seconds.
- **Baseten**: consistently the priciest (A100 $4.00/hr, H100 $6.50/hr, B200 $9.98/hr) — has minimum awake-time billing even with scale-to-zero.

**Recommendation:** Modal remains the right choice for this project's custom FLUX/ControlNet/gsplat containers — Python-native, true per-second billing, good scale-to-zero, and the team already has working deploys. Consider Fal.ai only if/when a workload maps cleanly onto one of their packaged models (e.g., calling Seedance/Kling/Veo passthrough) — no reason to self-host video models there. Do not move to Replicate/Baseten/RunPod for the core hero-render pipeline; RunPod is worth a look only for a cheap always-on splat-training dev loop.

## Cost per unit of the pipeline

**Hero render (FLUX.1-dev + ControlNet on A100, self-hosted Modal):** ~$0.01-0.02/render, confirmed by project's own measurement and consistent with Modal's ~$0.0007/s A100 rate at ~15-30s warm inference. FLUX.2-dev on H200 (2-4x cost per the CLAUDE.md note) would land ~$0.02-0.08/render — still cheap, gate stays about quality/latency not cost.

**Video generation — this is the dominant per-unit cost in the whole pipeline:**
- **API options** (verified Jul 2026 rates): Veo 3 Standard $0.40/s ($3.20/8s clip), Veo 3 Fast $0.15/s ($1.20/8s clip) — ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing. Kling 3.0/O3 $0.075-0.15/s depending on resolution/audio ($0.38-2.25 per 5-15s clip) — kling.ai/dev/pricing. Seedance 2.0 ranges $0.074-0.303/s across providers depending on resolution/speed tier ($0.37-3.03 per 5-10s clip) — fal.ai, evolink.ai, wavespeed.
- **Self-hosted option (Wan 2.2, Apache 2.0, open weights):** on Modal A100, a 5-sec 720p clip takes ~523s per one community benchmark (runaihome.com) — that's ~$0.36 in raw A100-seconds, comparable to or cheaper than Kling/Seedance API rates once amortized, though at much higher latency (~9 min vs seconds for API calls) and needing in-house pipeline maintenance. On H100 the same clip drops to ~284s (~$0.31). Wan 2.2's TI2V-5B variant is faster (~9 min for a 5s 720p clip on a single consumer GPU) but produces lower-fidelity output than the 14B MoE variant.
- **Verdict:** self-hosting Wan on Modal only wins economically at high volume (saves ~$0.05-2/clip vs API) but costs real engineering time and latency; for an MVP, start with Veo 3 Fast ($0.15/s, i.e. ~$1.20/8s) or Kling O3 720p ($0.075/s, ~$0.60/8s) as the metered pass-through cost, then evaluate self-hosting once video volume/user justifies the build.

**Gaussian splat reconstruction bake (100-200 img splatfacto run):** Consumer-GPU benchmarks (multiple sources) show 30k-iteration splatfacto training completes in 8-25 min depending on GPU (RTX 4090 ~12 min, A100 ~10 min, H100 ~8 min). At Modal's A100 rate that's **~$0.25-0.30 per splat bake** — startlingly cheap. Emerging fast methods (FastGS, CVPR 2026) claim 77-100s convergence (~$0.01-0.02/bake on A100) but are not yet mainstream-integrated into nerfstudio/gsplat as of this research. Recommend budgeting $0.30-0.50/bake (including COLMAP preprocessing overhead, retries, and safety margin) rather than the theoretical floor.

**Image-to-3D asset generation** (for entourage: trees, furniture, people): Meshy ~$0.10-0.30/model (credit-based, $0.01/credit equivalent), Tripo AI ~$0.10-0.30/model, Rodin (Hyper3D) premium quality at ~$0.50-1.50/model but requires $120/mo minimum subscription just for API access — 3daistudio.com survey (Mar 2026). Cheapest credible pick for bulk entourage generation: Meshy or Tripo, not Rodin, unless quality bar demands it for hero assets.

**LLM copilot loop (Claude API, verified Jul 2026):** Sonnet 4.6 $3/$15 per M input/output tokens; Opus 4.8 $5/$25; Haiku 4.5 $1/$5; Fable 5 (flagship) $10/$50 — platform.claude.com/docs/about-claude/pricing. A typical copilot turn (read scene state + tool calls + short response) is likely 2-5K tokens in, 500-1500 out — call it ~$0.02-0.04/turn on Sonnet 4.6, ~$0.005-0.01/turn on Haiku. At even heavy usage (50 copilot turns/user/month) that's $1-2/user/month — a rounding error next to render/video costs. Use Haiku or a cheap relay for high-frequency tool-routing, reserve Sonnet/Opus for actual design reasoning.

**Image generation (entourage/materials, FLUX API):** FLUX.1 Dev via Fal/Replicate ~$0.02-0.03/image; FLUX.1 Pro ~$0.04-0.06/image; FLUX.1 Schnell (Apache 2.0, self-hostable) ~$0.003-0.01/image or free if self-hosted. Trivial cost relative to video/splat.

## Business shape: pricing model recommendation

**Cost basis for the stated usage profile (50 hero renders + 10 video clips + 2 splat bakes/user/month):**
- 50 hero renders × $0.015 avg = **$0.75**
- 10 video clips × ~$0.60 avg (Kling O3 720p, 8s) = **$6.00** (or ~$3.60 if self-hosted Wan at scale, or ~$12-32 if Veo 3 Standard used instead of Fast/Kling)
- 2 splat bakes × $0.40 avg = **$0.80**
- LLM copilot overhead: ~$1-2
- **Total variable COGS: ~$8.55-10.55/user/month** (video-dominated; the number swings 3-5x depending solely on which video API/tier is chosen)

**Direct competitor pricing anchor — Veras (Chaos-owned, EvolveLAB), the closest existing product to this vision** (chaos.com/veras, evolvelab.io, verified 2026): Standard ~$29-59/mo for 500-2000 credits (image+video shared pool), effective ~$0.06-0.10/render to the end user. Ultra tier $348+/yr for 6000 credits. This is direct proof that AI-render-on-your-own-3D-model is already a live SaaS category with $30-60/mo per-seat pricing accepted by the market.

**Incumbent renderer pricing** (verified 2026): Enscape Solo $574.80/yr (~$48/mo), Premium $634.80/yr (~$53/mo) — chaos.com. Lumion Pro $1,149/yr (~$96/mo), Lumion View $229/yr (~$19/mo), Lumion Studio $1,499/yr (~$125/mo) — lumion.com. D5 Render Pro $360/yr or $38/mo, Teams $708/yr/seat — d5render.com. Twinmotion free under $1M firm revenue, ~$445/yr above.

**Recommended pricing model:**
1. **Entry/Pro tier: $39-49/mo per seat**, bundling ~40-60 hero renders + 5-8 video clips + 1-2 splat bakes/month, positioned directly against Veras and D5 Pro (undercuts Enscape, dramatically undercuts Lumion). At the profile above, COGS of ~$8.55-10.55 against $39-49 revenue yields **~73-82% gross margin** — healthy for a usage-capped SaaS, in line with typical vertical-AI-tool margins (60-85%).
2. **Overage/credit top-ups** beyond the monthly cap, priced at ~2-3x raw COGS (e.g., $1.50-2/extra video clip, $0.05/extra render) — mirrors Veras's and Maket's credit-topup patterns.
3. **Studio/Team tier: $79-99/seat/mo or floating license ~$1,000-1,500/yr**, higher caps, matching Lumion Studio/Chaos suite pricing psychology for firms already paying that range.
4. **Cost control levers to protect margin:** default new users to Kling O3 or Veo 3 Fast (not Veo 3 Standard, which is 3-8x more expensive per clip) for video; batch/cache splat bakes (expensive step is COLMAP + iteration count, not raw compute) at the theoretical floor ($0.30-0.50) already; route copilot tool-calls through Haiku 4.5 and reserve Sonnet/Opus for genuine reasoning turns; watch the Modal non-preemptible multiplier claim — if real for any always-on service, avoid always-on infra entirely and lean harder into scale-to-zero.

**Biggest margin risk:** video is 60-70% of variable COGS at the stated usage level and has the widest price variance across providers (3-8x between cheapest and priciest tier of the same underlying capability). The single highest-leverage cost-engineering decision is which video tier/provider is the *default* in the product, not GPU choice, splat optimization, or LLM model — those are all sub-$1/user/month regardless of choice.

## Adversarial stress-test (Opus 4.8)

**Verdict:** viable-with-changes

**Fatal flaws:**

- FATAL TO THE PRODUCT PROMISE, NOT THE ECONOMICS: The economics analysis quietly proves the incumbents win. Its own anchor — Veras — is owned by Chaos and, as of the May 2026 Enscape release, is now bundled FREE on ALL Enscape plans including Solo, running Nano Banana 2, with viewport ideation, image-to-video, Smart Selection one-click region targeting, Modify/Replace region edits, and Remove-Background compositing. D5 Render 3.0 (Mar 2026) ships 15+ AI features: AI Style Transfer, AI Inpainting (region edits), AI Image-to-3D (entourage), AI Scene Match agent (natural-language mood). The researcher's ENTIRE differentiated feature set — direct-manipulation region material swaps, entourage gen, copilot agent, image-to-video — already ships INSIDE the incumbents at their existing seat price, with the one thing the startup cannot replicate: it lives in the same file as the model, so 'move the window 2 ft' propagates automatically. The pricing recommendation ('undercut Enscape/Lumion, anchor against Veras') is self-refuting: you cannot undercut a feature that a competitor gives away for $0 as a bundled add-on to a renderer the architect already pays for and already has their model loaded in.
- FATAL TO PILLAR (c) 'Gaussian splat of the whole environment as EDITABLE 3D': Verified across 4 independent 2026 sources that this is not achievable at product quality for architecture specifically. (1) COLMAP/SfM + 3DGS fails on reflective glass and textureless surfaces — 'the majority of the building's exterior' in the cited glass-facade study — and on thin/repetitive elements (railings, mullions, balusters) where depth estimation destabilizes. Architecture is disproportionately glass + thin members. (2) Production reality (aukimi, Apr 2026): 'You cannot easily add geometry, change topology, or paint a splat the way you paint a mesh. The output is closer to a photo than to a model... The killer application — animated, lightable, fully editable splats — is still in research.' Editing/relighting/adding-objects in splats is all research-stage (3DSceneEditor, DeferredGS, REdiSplats — none production, most indoor-only). The product promise of 'architect can place/remove assets and art-direct the scene' in an editable splat does not exist as shippable tech. The economics report costed the BAKE ($0.30-0.50) but the bake produces a non-editable photo-proxy, not the editable 3D the vision sells.

**Risks:**

- Compounding-error blindspot: The report costs each stage in isolation (render $0.015, clip $0.60, bake $0.40) but never models the RETRY MULTIPLIER. Verified video-quality reviews (Kapwing/Stensyl/AVB, 2026) show even best-in-class Veo3/Kling3/Seedance2 exhibit geometry drift, nonsensical signage, style breaks, and 'the background changes significantly between halves' — on a fly-through of a SPECIFIC BUILDING that must stay geometrically faithful, this is disqualifying at first pass. Real COGS is (unit cost) x (attempts to acceptance). At even 3-5x reroll rate on video to get a client-showable clip that doesn't warp the mullions, the $6/user video COGS becomes $18-30, and 73-82% margin collapses toward or below break-even at $39-49.
- The 50-render/10-clip/2-bake monthly usage profile is asserted, not evidenced, and it's the load-bearing assumption for the entire margin claim. Verified archviz-workflow sources show real projects run 2-3 revision rounds over 1-2 weeks with 3-5 stills per project — heavy users (competition/marketing shops) will blow past 10 clips; light users (most architects, who buy the seat for one pitch/quarter) sit at near-zero and the $39-49 SaaS looks expensive vs a pay-per-use Veras credit pack. A flat usage assumption hides that the population is bimodal, which breaks flat-rate SaaS margins at both tails.
- Video-tier default recommendation (Veo3 Fast / Kling O3 720p) trades the exact dimension architecture needs. The report picks the cheap tier to protect margin, but the quality reviews show the cheap/fast tiers are where drift, artifacts, and consistency failures concentrate — precisely fatal for a fly-through that must preserve a real design. The dominant margin lever the researcher identifies (video tier) is in direct tension with the dominant quality requirement (geometric fidelity), and the report resolves it toward cost, not quality.
- Self-hosted Wan2.2 economics understated the good news but ignored the operational tax: the cited 523s/A100 is UNOPTIMIZED FP16; Lightning-LoRA distillation (verified) cuts A100 to ~146s (~$0.10/clip) — so the cost floor is better than claimed. BUT it comes with a quality drop (limb jitter, artifacts) and, critically, self-hosting means the startup owns every video failure and reroll, whereas API pass-through externalizes model risk. The 'later-stage optimization' framing is right, but the report doesn't flag that Wan can't do the multi-shot character/scene consistency that Kling/Seedance market as their headline — so self-hosting locks you out of the one capability that would reduce reroll rate.
- Modal '3x non-preemptible / 1.25x region' multiplier remains unverified and the report correctly flags it — but it matters more than the report implies IF any component (splat-training dev loop, warm video pool to cut latency, an always-on copilot) becomes non-scale-to-zero. The report's own latency data (Wan ~2.5-9 min/clip self-hosted) creates product pressure to keep a warm pool, which is exactly the always-on case where the multiplier, if real, bites.

**Recommendations:**

- Reframe the whole thesis: stop pricing to COMPETE with incumbents and price/position to INTEGRATE (the user's own parallel Pillar-e). The economics report's most defensible conclusion, once you fold in that Veras-in-Enscape is free and D5 has 15+ AI features, is that a standalone $39-49 SaaS is a losing wedge. The credible path is a plugin/copilot layer that sits ON TOP of Rhino/Enscape/D5 and does the ONE thing they don't: geometry-locked multi-view/video consistency from the live model (which the project already has partial IP on via reproject-from-3D + FLUX+ControlNet). Sell the hard part, not the commodity render.
- Kill or radically rescope Pillar (c). 'Editable Gaussian splat of the whole environment' is not shippable in 2026 for architecture (glass + thin elements + no true editing). Rescope to what IS real: splat as a non-editable BACKDROP/context stage composited around the real building mesh (which the project already built via Spark) — do not sell it as 'editable 3D the architect art-directs.' Move editable-scene ambition to the mesh side where the project already has semantic IDs.
- Model COGS as unit-cost x acceptance-attempts, not unit cost. Before any pricing commitment, run a live test: how many video generations does it take to get ONE client-showable fly-through of a specific building that a professional would accept without geometry warping? Multiply every video COGS number by that factor. If it's >3x, the $39-49 tier does not clear margin and video must be a metered pay-per-use overage, never bundled.
- Split the SaaS into metered/pay-per-use for video+splat and flat-rate only for the cheap deterministic stuff (hero renders, copilot). The verified per-project archviz cadence (2-3 revisions, 3-5 stills) and bimodal user base make flat-rate video a margin trap. Mirror Veras's credit-topup model the report already noticed — but make credits the DEFAULT for the expensive/high-variance stages, not the overflow.
- Verify the Modal multiplier before it enters any deck (report is right) AND commit to a hard rule: no always-on GPU. If product latency (2.5-9 min self-hosted video) forces a warm pool, keep it on a per-second scale-to-zero host and eat the cold-start, or stay on API pass-through until volume proves out — do not build the always-on infra the multiplier would punish.

**Notes:** The economics report is technically competent and its arithmetic mostly survives verification: Modal rates, Wan benchmarks (SaladCloud A100 523s / H100 284s confirmed exactly), video API prices, splat-bake times, and incumbent pricing all check out. As a cost-of-goods study it is sound, and its top recommendation (Modal backbone, meter video, default to cheap video tier, verify the Modal multiplier) is reasonable WITHIN its scope. My red-team verdict is viable-with-changes, not not-viable, because the pipeline is genuinely cheap to run — that part is real. But 'cheap to run' is answering the wrong question. Two things the report was scoped away from break the grand idea as stated: (1) The report's own competitive anchor, Veras, is now FREE inside Enscape and D5 ships 15+ AI features — the differentiated feature set already ships inside the incumbents, in-file, at existing seat price. You cannot undercut free. (2) Pillar (c), the editable-splat-of-the-whole-environment, is not shippable tech for architecture in 2026 — glass/thin-element SfM failure plus no true splat editing (all research-stage), verified across four sources. Additionally the report costs stages in isolation and ignores the reroll multiplier that video-consistency failures force, which is where the 73-82% margin actually lives or dies. The optimism survives as a COST model and dies as a PRODUCT model. The salvage is real: sell geometry-locked consistency (the hard IP the project already partly owns) as an integration layer, meter the high-variance stages, and demote splats to backdrop.
