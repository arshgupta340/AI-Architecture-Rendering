# competitive-market — research + stress-test (2026-07-03)

**Headline:** The AI-arch-viz market is crowded with cheap, thin "photo-in-photo-out" tools racing to a floor of $20-40/mo, while the real strategic event of 2025-26 is Chaos (V-Ray/Enscape) buying the category leader (Veras) and folding AI directly into the incumbent render pipeline — meaning "another AI render tool" is not differentiated unless it solves the two things nobody has: cross-view/3D consistency and true non-destructive, direct-manipulation editing tied to a live 3D model.

## Key facts

- Chaos (V-Ray/Enscape/Corona parent) acquired EvolveLAB/Veras in Feb 2025 and fully integrated Veras AI into Enscape/V-Ray/Corona with unified licensing by May 2026 — source: chaos.com/press official release + engineering.com + seenews.com corroboration.
- Veras pricing: ~$59/mo named license, ~$51/mo floating annual ($612/yr), $12.42/mo student ($149/yr); some 2026 reviews cite a lower ~$29-39/mo post-Chaos entry tier — source: evolvelab.io/veras, aitoolsbakery.com review.
- Veras's #1 documented complaint is cross-view/cross-angle material-and-lighting inconsistency, confirmed independently by a dedicated 2026 review and live EvolveLAB support-forum threads (May 2026) — the same multi-view-drift problem this project is already fighting.
- ArkoAI: $39/mo Pro, Revit/Rhino/SketchUp cloud plugin, hands-on test (Hayne Architects, Jan 2026) found prompts frequently ignored and materials inaccurate out-of-the-box; ~22K monthly site visits, 2-person company per Exa traffic data.
- LookX: $20/mo individual ($199/yr), $269/mo team (5 seats); independent reviews confirm seed-locking for consistency 'didn't quite lock' and style-transfer is 'too literal' (color bleed from reference photos) — search interest down ~75% from 2023 peak.
- ArchiVinci: 761K+ claimed registered users, $25-79/mo or one-time payments ($49-599); a direct 2026 competitor comparison (nuit.archi) frames it as 'independent stateless modules' vs. Nuit's 'one coherent project with branching history' — closest philosophical analog to this project's non-destructive-layer thesis found in the market.
- mnml.ai: 2.4M+ users claimed, $29/mo Pro / $99/mo Unlimited, credit-based — part of a commoditized cluster (Rendershop, Spacely AI, Rendair AI) all pricing $20-40/mo for photo/sketch-to-render.
- Spline AI is a general web/3D-object generator, NOT an architecture tool; Arcway pivoted in 2026 away from AI rendering entirely into an end-to-end homebuilder sales/configurator platform, explicitly marketing 'the rendering era is over' — a signal that standalone AI-viz may not be a durable business alone.
- Outsourced render pricing (2026): $50-150 basic exterior, $150-1,000+ high-end exterior, $100-700 interior, €400-5,000+ for European competition-grade; animation $500-6,000+ depending on length — sources: render4tomorrow.com, 4dviz.com.
- Real-time render engine seat pricing has converged to $30-90/mo range with AI features now bundled free (Enscape ~$85/mo, V-Ray ~$43/yr entry, Twinmotion free under $1M revenue, D5 free tier) — this sets the effective price ceiling for any new AI-viz entrant.
- 74% of US architecture firms are sole proprietors or have <3 architectural staff (AIA Small Firm Compensation Report, Feb 2026); only 46% of small/solo firms use AI tools vs 78% of 50+-staff firms, with hardware cost ($5,000-15,000+/workstation) cited as the barrier; 48% of designers overall cite 'unreliable results,' not cost, as the top AI-adoption barrier (Chaos/Architizer 2026 Pulse survey).

## Recommendation

Do not compete as "another AI render button" — that tier is commoditized at $20-40/mo and the incumbent (Chaos/Enscape/V-Ray) now bundles equivalent AI stylization for firms that already pay for real-time rendering, which is most mid-size+ firms. Differentiate on the two things zero competitors credibly deliver: (1) genuine multi-view/scene consistency — this is the industry's most-cited unsolved complaint (confirmed at Veras, LookX, ArchiVinci) and this project's reproject-from-3D work is a real head start worth pushing hard rather than treating as a side quest; (2) a persistent, non-destructive, region-addressable project model rather than stateless per-image generation — Nuit (nuit.archi) is the only competitor moving this direction and should be tracked closely as the nearest rival, not dismissed. Price should not anchor below the Chaos-bundled ~$50-90/mo tier since that's nearly free for existing V-Ray/Enscape seat-holders; instead price for the capability gap. Treat Arcway's pivot away from pure rendering as a cautionary signal, not proof of a dead category — it suggests "just a render tool" doesn't sustain a business alone, reinforcing the case for pursuing the fuller "editable environment + copilot" vision rather than a narrower renderer-only wedge.

## Open questions

- Nuit (nuit.archi) launched mid-2026 with a directly competing 'coherent project, not stateless modules' thesis — worth a dedicated follow-up deep-dive on its funding, traction, and whether it has solved multi-view consistency or only project/reference coherence within a single generation session.
- Could not verify current SketchUp Diffusion (Trimble) pricing, adoption numbers, or user complaints in depth — only found it referenced as an in-viewport AI Lab feature; a follow-up search specifically on Trimble/SketchUp Labs AI Render would sharpen the 'incumbent absorbs AI' pattern.
- No solid data found on Recraft V3, Magnific, Qwen-Image-Edit, or HiDream-E1 specifically as architecture-viz competitors (these appear in the project's own B2 bake-off as general image models, not as arch-specific product competitors) — unclear if any startup has packaged them into an arch-specific offering not yet surfaced by this search.
- Traction/revenue figures for nearly every tool are self-reported (user counts, ratings) with no independently verifiable revenue, funding, or paying-customer data — actual market size and startup survival rates in this category remain unverified.
- No direct competitor found combining Gaussian-splat environment editing with architecture-specific material/entourage direct-manipulation (this project's stated differentiator) — worth a dedicated follow-up search closer to product launch to confirm this whitespace still holds, since the space (Marble, Gracia AI, Nucleus4D, Waldek) is moving fast.

## Competitive map (verified 2026 pricing/positioning)

**Veras (EvolveLAB)** — the category leader, and the single most important fact in this space: **Chaos (owner of V-Ray, Corona, Enscape) acquired EvolveLAB in Feb 2025** and by May 2026 had integrated Veras directly into every version of Enscape/V-Ray/Corona with unified licensing (chaos.com/press, May 27 2026 release). Pricing: named license ~$59/mo, floating ~$51/mo ($612/yr), student $12.42/mo ($149/yr); other reviews cite a lower ~$29-39/mo entry tier post-Chaos-bundling. Model-aware (renders from your actual geometry via a "Geometry Override" slider, not pure prompt-to-image), plugs into Revit/SketchUp/Rhino/Vectorworks/Archicad/Forma. #1 complaint, confirmed across a dedicated review and the EvolveLAB support forum: **cross-view/cross-angle consistency** — the same building rendered from two angles gets different material interpretations; forum threads (May 2026) also show live breakage from a "Chaos Credits" pricing migration. This is *our exact hard problem* (multi-view drift), now confirmed as the industry's #1 documented pain point at the market leader. [chaos.com/press/chaos-acquires-evolvelab-and-its-aec-ai-tools], [aitoolsbakery.com/blog/veras-review], [forum.evolvelab.io]

**ArkoAI** — Revit/Rhino/SketchUp plugin, cloud-based, "Active View" render-in-place. $39/mo Pro (SourceForge/archaitool corroborate), free tier capped at 608px/800kb. Two modes: "Render" (geometry-locked, materials/lighting only) and "Ideate" (geometry can drift) — same render/ideate split we already use. Independent hands-on test (Hayne Architects, Jan 2026): prompts were frequently ignored, landscaping/site elements didn't translate, materials felt "off" — underwhelming out-of-the-box, steep learning curve, only 2 employees per Exa web-traffic data (~22K monthly visits, declining). Small, low-traction player.

**LookX** — web-based, credit system ($20/mo individual, $269/mo team-5-seat, $199/yr). Independent reviews (toolsforhumans.ai, sanazvazirian.ca) converge on the same verdict: **prompt-fidelity is good but consistency/seed-locking doesn't actually lock** — "it didn't quite lock... results shifted anyway." Style-transfer is "too literal" (bled lavender-purple from a reference photo onto stone/windows). Google Trends shows search interest down ~75% from a mid-2023 peak — hype phase over, small stable niche. Direct evidence that seed/consistency claims across this entire category are frequently marketing, not working features.

**ArchiVinci** — 761K+ registered users claimed, module-based (Interior/Exterior/Exact-Render/Masterplan/Dream/360°HDRI/video), built on Stable Diffusion+ControlNet. Premium $25-79/mo or one-time payments ($49/3-day to $599/1-yr) — unusual one-time-payment option in the category. Two sharply different reviews: a 9.04/10 consensus site calls it best-in-class for sketch-to-render speed; a competitor teardown (Rendershop) calls its default text-to-design output "illustrative, not photorealistic." Most useful finding: a head-to-head against **Nuit** (nuit.archi, mid-2026 launch) frames the exact strategic fork our project sits on — ArchiVinci is "independent stateless modules," Nuit is "one coherent project" where exterior/plan/interior share references and a branching exploration tree. Nuit is the closest thing to a direct philosophical competitor to non-destructive, project-coherent editing — worth monitoring directly. [nuit.archi/blog/nuit-vs-archivinci]

**mnml.ai** — 2.4M+ "designers" claimed, $29/mo Pro, $99/mo Unlimited, credit-based (10 credits/main generation), rated 4.8-8.06/10 depending on source. Positioned as exterior rendering + virtual staging; competes at the low end alongside Rendershop, Spacely AI, Rendair AI — an increasingly commoditized cluster all doing sketch/photo-to-render at $20-40/mo.

**MyArchitectAI, SketchUp Diffusion, Spline AI, Arcway** — MyArchitectAI is a small "renders in under 10 seconds" also-ran, frequently listed as an ArchiVinci alternative but with no independent depth found. SketchUp Diffusion is Trimble's own in-viewport AI render Lab feature (incumbent absorbing AI directly, same pattern as Chaos/Veras). **Spline AI is NOT an arch tool** — it's a general web/3D-object generator (game assets, UI elements); irrelevant to this market despite name recognition. **Arcway has pivoted away from rendering entirely** — as of mid-2026 it rebranded as an end-to-end **homebuilder sales/configurator platform** ("buyers walk the plan, swap finishes, watch price update live"), explicitly positioning against "render labs" as obsolete ("The rendering era is over"). This is a meaningful signal: one arch-adjacent AI-viz startup already concluded that standalone AI rendering isn't a durable business and moved downstream into the transaction (real-estate sales), not upstream into design tooling.

**World-model / Gaussian-splat entrants** — no direct "architect edits a splat of their own model" competitor found. Adjacent activity: World Labs' Marble (general spatially-consistent 3D world generation, not arch-specific), Matterport (acquired by CoStar for ~$1.6B, 2025) dominates real-estate splat/photogrammetry capture but is a documentation tool, not a design/edit tool. Gracia AI ($1.7M raised, 4D Gaussian splatting for VFX/XR, not AEC), Nucleus4D ($1.5M pre-seed, real-estate virtual tours via splats, Zillow-approved), Waldek/Gauzilla Pro ($120K seed, browser-native no-code splat editor, AEC-adjacent PoCs with Tesla/VW reported but unverified). **None of these let an architect direct-manipulate materials/entourage on a splat of their own design** — this remains genuine whitespace, not yet claimed.

## Market reality: what firms actually pay

- **Outsourced renders** (per-image, professional studios): basic exterior $50-150, standard $150-400, high-end $400-1,000+; interior $100-700; competition/developer-grade in Europe €400-2,500 (up to €5,000+ for complex commercial). Animation: $500-2,000 for 30-60s, $2,000-6,000 for 1-2min, $6,000+ cinematic. [render4tomorrow.com, 4dviz.com]
- **In-house cost**: 3D artist salary $800-3,000+/mo + software $100-300/mo + hardware amortization $100-500/mo = **$1,200-4,500+/mo per artist**, vs. outsourcing 5-10 renders/mo for $1,000-3,000 total.
- **Software seats (2026, verified)**: Enscape ~$85/mo Solo; Twinmotion free under $1M revenue / $445/yr; D5 Render free tier / ~$40/mo Pro; Lumion $229/yr (View) to $1,149/yr (Pro); V-Ray ~$43/yr Solo (Chaos undercuts hard as an entry hook now that AI is bundled in); Corona ~$33.50/mo; Octane ~$20/mo Prime. **Real-time renderers have converged toward $30-90/mo** with AI features bundled free — this is the price ceiling any new entrant is implicitly competing against.
- **Buyer segment**: 74% of US architecture firms are sole proprietors or have <3 architectural staff (AIA Small Firm Report, Feb 2026); 49% of surveyed A&E firms have ≤10 employees. AI adoption: 60% of firms use AI somewhere, but only 46% of small/solo firms do (vs. 78% of 50+-staff firms) — hardware/per-seat cost is explicitly cited as the small-firm barrier ($5,000-15,000+/workstation for local GPU-heavy tools). **48% of designers cite "unreliable results" as the #1 adoption barrier**, ahead of cost. [aiopsnav.ai citing Chaos/Architizer 2026 Pulse, AIA 2025 Tech Survey]

## Whitespace and blunt assessment

The category has commoditized around "upload a photo/sketch, get a stylized render back" at $20-40/mo, and the #1 complaint across *every* tool tested (Veras, LookX, ArchiVinci) is the same: **consistency — across views, across sessions, across revisions.** That is precisely the hard problem this project has already identified and is actively fighting (multi-view drift, full-360 failure). No competitor has solved it; Veras's own support forum shows active user reports of it in mid-2026. Solving multi-view consistency robustly would be a genuine, defensible differentiator, not a marketing claim like the others'.

Second, the market's most consequential move wasn't a startup — it was Chaos buying the leading independent AI-render plugin and bundling it for free-ish into Enscape/V-Ray, which almost every mid-size and large firm already has a seat for. That structurally caps the price and the standalone-tool addressable market: firms with existing Chaos/Enscape seats have "good enough" AI rendering for free already. A standalone renderer competing on stylization alone is fighting a war Chaos has already started winning by distribution, not quality.

Third, "another AI render tool" is not differentiated. What nobody offers — confirmed by scanning every module-based competitor (ArchiVinci explicitly criticized for being "stateless modules" with no project coherence) — is **non-destructive, region-addressable, direct-manipulation editing bound to one persistent 3D-consistent scene** that an architect can keep revising over weeks (this project's core thesis). Nuit is the one competitor explicitly building toward "one coherent project," and is worth tracking closely as the nearest philosophical rival. Arcway's pivot away from rendering into homebuilder sales tooling is a warning sign that pure-viz plays may not sustain a business alone — but it also vacates the "AI copilot embedded in a real editable 3D design tool for architects" niche, which nobody currently owns.

**Product implication**: position explicitly against both axes — (1) "we solve the multi-view/360 consistency problem the $30-tools don't," verified via the reproject-from-3D pipeline already built, and (2) "we are the non-destructive layer, not a stateless render button," matching what Nuit is attempting but grounded in a real BIM-sourced 3D model (Rhino) rather than text-only generation. Price anchored below Chaos-bundled Enscape/V-Ray (~$50-90/mo) is not a winning axis alone since that's now nearly free for firms with existing Chaos seats — differentiate on capability (consistency + editability + splat-environment context), not on undercutting price into the $20-tool commodity tier.

## Adversarial stress-test (Opus 4.8)

**Verdict:** viable-with-changes

**Fatal flaws:**

- none

**Risks:**

- Same-primitive incumbent, not a laggard. Verified: Chaos bundled Veras into ALL Enscape plans incl. Solo by May 2026 (single installer, credit model), and Enscape is 'used by 85 of the top 100 architecture firms.' Worse than the researcher stated: Veras's render engine is now Google Nano Banana Pro — the EXACT model this project's hero pipeline depends on. The incumbent has the same render primitive as the grand idea AND owns distribution to the installed base. Any wedge that competes on render quality alone loses on distribution before it starts.
- The 'multi-view consistency is unsolved whitespace' differentiator is eroding under the project's feet. 2026 practitioner sources report Nano Banana Pro reference-image conditioning already yielding 'material consistency perfect' across angles, via a simple per-project reference-library prompt pattern — no proprietary 3D reproject required. The project's reproject-from-3D is more rigorous for hard cases, but it is racing a foundation-model capability improving monthly and shipping free inside Enscape/Veras. A moat built on 'we solve consistency' has a shrinking half-life.
- The 'non-destructive coherent project' differentiator is already CLAIMED and executing, not open whitespace. Nuit (nuit.archi) is live, shipping branching + lineage + style-coherence across exterior/plan/interior in one project tree, and aggressively content-marketing against every competitor (ArchiVinci, mnml, Flora, Maket). The researcher framed Nuit as a rival 'to monitor'; it is in fact a direct competitor already delivering the exact thesis the recommendation leans on. Nuit's gap: it is text/image-first, NOT bound to a real BIM/Rhino 3D model — which is the only remaining defensible seam.
- Editable-Gaussian-splat differentiator is real product whitespace but academically crowded and technically unproven at product quality. 3DSceneEditor (WACV 2026), Splatshop, ArtisanGS, and aioli-gs-toolkit (a V-Ray->splat archviz capture pipeline!) show the primitives are commoditizing in open source. Semantic splat editing works in papers/alphas; nobody has shipped it as a reliable architect-facing product. This is the freshest moat AND the highest execution/quality risk — the compounding-error concern (capture -> train -> segment -> edit -> re-render coherently) is real and unvalidated.
- Price/economics squeeze is confirmed. Verified Enscape Solo ~$87/mo / ~$575/yr with Veras AI now included; V-Ray entry undercut hard; Twinmotion/D5 free tiers. For the ~50%+ of the market on an existing Chaos seat, 'good enough' AI render is now a sunk cost. A standalone tool must justify a NET-NEW line item on capability incumbents structurally cannot match, not on being cheaper or marginally better.
- Workflow-reality risk: the 'move the window 2ft after renders' fee-erosion problem the thesis targets is genuinely the #1 pain (multiple 2026 sources confirm), but the market answer is already arriving — Snaptrude Present Mode ('make the change in the meeting') and Enscape Live Updates give model-tied live views today. The project's non-destructive edit layer is differentiated only if it does something these cannot: region-addressable material/entourage edits on a photoreal render that stay coherent through geometry changes — which is exactly the hard, unvalidated part.

**Recommendations:**

- Kill 'we're the better AI render button' entirely. Verified fact: the incumbent's render engine IS Nano Banana Pro, distributed free to 85 of the top-100 firms. Do not anchor the wedge on render quality or on multi-view consistency as a standalone claim — both are being commoditized by the same foundation model the project itself uses.
- Anchor the whole product on the one seam nobody else holds: binding non-destructive, region-addressable editing to a REAL BIM/Rhino 3D model with per-element semantic IDs (which the web3d-prototype already has). Nuit owns 'coherent project' but is text/image-first with no true geometry; Enscape/Veras owns render+distribution but its AI layer is prompt-driven ideation, not a persistent editable project model tied to live geometry. The intersection — geometry-locked + non-destructive + region-addressable + survives 'move the window 2ft' — is the only defensible position. Build the demo around a post-render geometry change propagating cleanly, because that is the fee-erosion moment the whole industry is trying to solve.
- Treat Nuit as the primary competitor, not Veras. Do a hands-on teardown of Nuit's branching model within one week and define the explicit capability it structurally cannot reach (real 3D-model binding). If Nuit ships Rhino/Revit geometry binding, the seam closes — this is the single biggest strategic watch item.
- Reframe the incumbent from 'enemy' to 'distribution channel' — this validates the grand idea's parallel track (b): integrate AI assistance INTO Enscape/V-Ray/D5 rather than only competing. Given Chaos owns the render+distribution, a Rhino/Enscape-side plugin that adds the persistent editable-project layer on top of their render output may be a faster wedge than a standalone app fighting their distribution.
- De-risk the editable-Gaussian-splat pillar before betting the roadmap on it. It is the freshest moat but product-unproven; run a narrow feasibility spike (capture->segment->edit->re-render one real Rhino scene end-to-end at presentation quality) before treating it as a headline feature. If compounding error across those stages can't hit client-deliverable quality, demote it to a 'context backdrop' feature (which the Spark work already does well) rather than a core editing surface.
- Price above the $20-40 commodity floor AND explicitly above the Chaos-bundled ~$50-90 tier, justified by the editability/geometry-binding capability gap — but be honest that TAM is capped: 74% of firms are <3 staff and only 46% of small firms use AI at all, citing 'unreliable results' (48%) over cost as the top barrier. The product must be reliable-at-product-quality, not demo-quality, or it dies on the exact objection the market already voices.

**Notes:** Verified the load-bearing claims via web search; all held, and two came back WORSE for the grand idea than the researcher wrote. (1) Chaos-Veras bundling is real and now spans all Enscape plans incl. Solo — but critically Veras's engine is Nano Banana Pro, the same model this project uses, so the incumbent is not behind on the render primitive and owns distribution to 85 of the top-100 firms. (2) The 'multi-view consistency is unsolved whitespace' differentiator is eroding: 2026 practitioners already get cross-angle material consistency from Nano Banana Pro reference conditioning via a prompt pattern, free inside Enscape. (3) The 'non-destructive coherent project' whitespace the recommendation leans on is already occupied — Nuit is live and executing that exact thesis, understated by the researcher as merely 'to monitor.' No flaw is fatal: the grand idea survives because one genuine seam remains — non-destructive, region-addressable editing bound to a REAL BIM/Rhino 3D model with semantic IDs, which the project already has and which neither Nuit (no true geometry) nor Veras (prompt ideation, not persistent editable project) holds. The competitive dimension does not break the idea, but it invalidates two of the three differentiators the researcher recommended leading with, and demands the wedge be repositioned onto geometry-binding + the parallel 'integrate into incumbents' track. Verdict: viable-with-changes — the market is harsher and the moats thinner than the report concluded, but a defensible position still exists if the pitch is geometry-locked editability, not render quality or consistency-as-a-standalone-claim.
